# Commercial use

**Short version: personal, educational, research, charitable and government use is free. Using this inside a company, or inside something you sell, needs a licence.**

This is not a feature gate. The licence decides *who may use the software commercially*, not what the software does — there is one build, and it is identical either way.

## When you need one

| Situation | Licence |
| :--- | :--- |
| Personal project, study, hobby, or testing with no commercial purpose in mind | Not needed |
| Teaching, academic research, a charity, a public research body, a public safety or health body, or a government institution | Not needed |
| A company rolling it out internally — even only to redact its own contracts | **Needed** |
| Shipping it inside a product or service you sell | **Needed** |
| A paid platform you operate for other organisations | **Needed** |
| Consultancy, agency or legal work delivered to paying clients | **Needed** |

The dividing line is not "did money change hands for this software". It is whether the *use* serves a commercial purpose — which is why a company using it free of charge internally still needs a licence.

The licence text itself is deliberately specific about the free cases: [PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0](../LICENSE) names personal use, charitable organisations, educational institutions, public research organisations, public safety and health organisations, environmental protection organisations and government institutions as permitted regardless of how they are funded.

## What a commercial licence grants

- A perpetual, worldwide, **non-exclusive** right to use, modify and distribute the software for commercial purposes
- Cover for the company and its affiliates — including internal deployment, paid client work and embedding in a product you sell
- The same software, with no feature limits, no keys, no activation, no telemetry
- The version you licence, plus updates released while your licence is in force

Terms are agreed in writing for each licence. This page describes the standard offer; the signed agreement is what binds.

## What it costs

Quoted per request, as a single flat fee. The two things that move the number are how many people would use it and whether it ends up inside something you sell.

No subscription is required, and there is no per-seat metering. If the quoted figure does not work for you, say so — a smaller deployment usually has a smaller number, and the point is to be affordable enough that paying is easier than not paying.

## How to request one

Open a **[commercial licence request](https://github.com/5Never5/llm-privacy-gateway/issues/new?template=commercial-license.yml)**. Say how it would be used and roughly how many people are involved. From there the discussion moves to a private channel, and you get a written licence to hand to whoever needs to see it.

There is no sales process: no call, no purchase-order choreography, no demo booking. It is a one-person project and the paperwork is short on purpose.

**Already using it commercially without a licence?** Tell us rather than disappearing. The licence you would be accepting gives a 32-day window to come into compliance after written notice, and that is the spirit it is meant to be read in — a licence is easier to arrange than an argument.

## If you are not sure

Ask in the same place. Nobody has been charged for a question, and a straight answer is usually faster than an internal legal review. If it turns out you do not need a licence, that is a perfectly good outcome.

## Evaluation

For a company that wants to try it seriously before committing, a written evaluation permission can normally be arranged for a limited period at no charge — ask, and say what you want to test. That keeps a pilot inside the licence instead of in a grey area.
